## Ingredients

*Quantities below are suggested proportions based on my cooking method.*

**For the plain rice**

- 1 cup rice
- Water, according to the rice variety and cooking method

**For the chana dal**

- 3/4 cup chana dal, rinsed and soaked for 30–60 minutes
- 2 1/2 cups water, plus more as needed
- Salt, to taste

**For the dal tadka**

- 1 tbsp ghee
- 1/2 tsp mustard seeds
- 1–2 green chillies, slit
- 1 medium onion, finely chopped
- 1 medium tomato, chopped

**For the kala chana palak**

- 3/4 cup dried kala chana, soaked overnight and drained
- 300 g spinach, washed and finely chopped
- 1 medium onion, chopped
- 1/4 tsp turmeric powder
- Salt, to taste

**For the peanut, sesame, and coconut paste**

- 2 tbsp peanuts
- 1 tbsp white sesame seeds
- 1/4 cup grated coconut
- 2–3 garlic cloves
- 1 dried red chilli
- A little water, for grinding

**For the palak tadka**

- 1 tbsp mustard oil or coconut oil
- 1/2 tsp cumin seeds
- 2 garlic cloves, chopped
- 1 tsp chana dal
- 1 tsp split, hulled moong dal
- 8–10 curry leaves
- 1 green chilli, slit or chopped

## Method

1. **Pressure-cook the kala chana.** Place the soaked, drained kala chana in a pressure cooker with enough fresh water to cover it comfortably, following your cooker's minimum liquid requirements. Pressure-cook until tender, then let the pressure release naturally. The chana should squash easily when pressed. Drain, reserving a little of the cooking liquid.

2. **Cook the chana dal.** Drain the soaked chana dal and add it to a pressure cooker with 2 1/2 cups of fresh water. Cook until soft, then let the pressure release naturally. Stir, add salt, and adjust the consistency with a little hot water if needed.

3. **Make the ghee tadka.** Heat ghee in a pan. Add the mustard seeds and let them splutter, then add the green chillies. Add the onion and sauté until lightly golden. Stir in the tomato and cook until soft. Add this tadka to the cooked chana dal, mix well, and simmer for 3–5 minutes.

4. **Cook the rice.** Rinse the rice thoroughly and cook with the amount of water recommended for your rice variety. Once tender, let it rest covered for 5–10 minutes before fluffing gently.

5. **Grind the paste.** Blend the peanuts, white sesame seeds, grated coconut, garlic, and dried red chilli with a little water. Aim for a thick paste that is mostly fine but still has a little texture.

6. **Make the tadka for the palak.** Heat mustard oil or coconut oil in a wide pan. Add the cumin seeds, followed by the chana dal and moong dal. Cook over medium-low heat until the dals begin to turn golden. Add the garlic, curry leaves, and green chilli, and sauté briefly until fragrant, taking care not to burn the garlic.

7. **Add the onion and spinach.** Add the chopped onion and cook until softened. Stir in the turmeric and salt. Add the chopped spinach in batches, stirring as it wilts. Cook until it has softened and most of the released water has evaporated.

8. **Add the kala chana and paste.** Stir in the cooked kala chana and cook for 2–3 minutes. Add the peanut, sesame, and coconut paste. Mix thoroughly and cook over medium-low heat for 5–7 minutes, until the paste loses its raw aroma. Add a splash of the reserved chana cooking liquid if the mixture becomes too dry. Taste and adjust the salt.

9. **Serve.** Serve the plain rice with the hot chana dal and a generous helping of kala chana palak on the side.

---

**Tips:** Check the chana, not just the whistle count — my soaked kala chana softened in about one whistle, but cooking time varies with the cooker, soaking time, and age of the chana, so keep cooking if it's still firm. Keep some texture in the paste; a slightly coarse grind gives the spinach a lovely nutty texture. Cook the tadka dals gently so they turn golden without becoming dark or burnt. Add water sparingly to the palak, since spinach releases its own moisture — use the reserved chana liquid only as needed. I usually use mustard oil, but coconut oil works well with the coconut in the paste.
