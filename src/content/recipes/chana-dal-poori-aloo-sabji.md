## Ingredients

*Makes approximately 16–18 pooris.*

**For cooking the chana dal**

- 1 cup chana dal
- 1/4 tsp turmeric powder
- Salt, to taste
- Water, sufficient for pressure cooking according to your cooker's instructions

**For the chana dal filling**

- 1 1/2 tbsp mustard oil
- 1 tsp cumin seeds
- 2 green chillies, finely chopped
- 1/4 tsp turmeric powder
- 1/2 tsp red chilli powder, or to taste
- A pinch of hing
- Cooked chana dal
- Salt, if needed

**For the poori dough**

- 3 cups whole-wheat flour
- 1 tbsp ghee
- 1/2 tsp ajwain
- 1/2 tsp kalonji
- 3/4 tsp salt, or to taste
- Water, as needed
- Refined oil, for deep-frying

**For the aloo ki sabji**

- 4 medium potatoes
- 1 medium onion, finely chopped
- 1/2–3/4 cup fresh tomato purée
- 1 green chilli, chopped
- 1 tsp grated ginger
- 3 garlic cloves, finely chopped
- 1/2 tsp mustard seeds
- 1/2 tsp cumin seeds
- 6–8 curry leaves
- 1/2 tsp turmeric powder
- 1 tsp coriander powder
- 1/2 tsp cumin powder
- 2 tbsp oil
- Salt, to taste
- 1 1/2–2 cups water, adjusted to your preferred consistency
- Fresh coriander, chopped, to finish

## Method

1. **Cook the chana dal.** Rinse the chana dal thoroughly. Add it to a pressure cooker with turmeric, salt, and sufficient water, following your cooker's minimum liquid requirement. Pressure-cook until tender and let the pressure release naturally before opening. Once a small sample has cooled, check that the dal mashes easily between your fingers. Keep any remaining cooking water with the dal — it will be dried out in the kadhai while preparing the filling.

2. **Prepare the chana dal filling.** Heat the mustard oil in a kadhai over medium heat. Add the cumin seeds and let them splutter. Add the green chillies, followed by turmeric, red chilli powder, and hing. Stir briefly, taking care not to burn the spices. Add the cooked chana dal along with any remaining cooking water. Mix thoroughly and mash with the back of a sturdy spoon or a potato masher. Cook uncovered, stirring regularly, until the excess water evaporates. The filling should be dry enough to hold together when pressed, with no loose liquid. Taste and adjust the salt, then spread the filling on a plate and let it cool completely before stuffing.

3. **Prepare the dough.** Combine the whole-wheat flour, salt, ajwain, and kalonji in a large bowl. Add the ghee and rub it into the flour with your fingertips. Gradually add water, a little at a time, and knead into a smooth, firm but pliable dough. It should be firmer than chapati dough without feeling dry or cracking. Cover and rest for 20 minutes, then knead briefly again before dividing.

4. **Stuff and seal the pooris.** Divide the dough into 16–18 equal portions and roll each into a smooth ball, keeping them covered while you work. Flatten one ball into a small disc, keeping the centre slightly thicker than the edges. Place approximately 1 tablespoon of cooled filling in the centre, leaving the edges clear. Bring the edges up around the filling and pinch them firmly together to seal. Gently flatten the stuffed ball with your palm, keeping the seam facing down. Repeat with the remaining dough and filling. Avoid overstuffing, as this can cause the pooris to tear while rolling.

5. **Roll the pooris.** Lightly grease the rolling surface and rolling pin with oil. Gently roll each stuffed ball into a poori about 10–12 cm wide, using light, even pressure and turning it as you roll so the filling spreads evenly. Keep the pooris slightly thicker than plain pooris — if rolled too thin, the filling may break through the dough. Prepare a few at a time and keep them covered so they do not dry out.

6. **Fry the pooris.** Heat enough refined oil for deep-frying in a deep kadhai. Once hot, adjust the flame to low-medium, keeping the oil hot enough for steady bubbling. Test with a small piece of dough — it should rise steadily to the surface without browning immediately. Carefully slide one poori into the oil. As it begins to rise, gently press the surface with a slotted spoon to encourage it to puff. Turn it over and fry until both sides are evenly golden and cooked through. Lift it out, let the excess oil drip back into the kadhai, and transfer to a rack or paper-lined plate. Repeat with the remaining pooris, adjusting the heat as needed and avoiding overcrowding the kadhai.

7. **Prepare the aloo ki sabji.** Boil the potatoes until tender. Peel and break them into rough chunks, lightly crushing a few pieces to help thicken the gravy. Heat the oil in a kadhai. Add the mustard seeds and let them splutter, then add the cumin seeds and curry leaves. Add the onion and cook until softened and lightly golden. Stir in the green chilli, ginger, and garlic, and cook until fragrant. Add turmeric, coriander powder, and cumin powder. Stir briefly, then add the fresh tomato purée and salt. Cook, stirring occasionally, until the purée thickens and loses its raw smell.

8. **Simmer and finish.** Add the potatoes and gently mix to coat them in the masala. Pour in the water and simmer for 10–15 minutes, lightly mashing a few potato pieces into the gravy. Adjust the salt and consistency, then finish with fresh coriander.

9. **Serve.** Serve the chana dal pooris hot with the aloo ki sabji, with plenty of potato gravy for dipping.

---

**Tips:** Dry the filling thoroughly — cook off the excess water in the kadhai so the filling is easy to stuff. Cool the filling before stuffing, as warm filling can soften the dough and make rolling difficult. Keep the dough edges free of filling so they seal securely. Roll gently — light pressure helps prevent tearing and keeps the filling evenly distributed. Maintain the oil temperature: oil that is too cool makes the pooris greasy, while excessively hot oil browns the outside before the dough cooks through.
